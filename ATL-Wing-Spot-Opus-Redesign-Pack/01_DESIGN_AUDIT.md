# ATL Wing Spot Redesign Audit

## Core problem
The current redesign is technically polished but visually behaves like an AI-generated creative-agency restaurant concept instead of an unmistakable ATL Wing Spot brand experience.

## Highest-priority issues
1. The hero is heavily left-anchored. Giant text sits on the left, the food cutout floats on the right, and the two elements do not feel composed together.
2. Too many sections repeat the same formula: tiny eyebrow/index, huge display headline, paragraph, reveal animation, cards/panel.
3. The homepage is too long and too portfolio-like. It showcases design ideas before it helps a hungry customer order.
4. The copy sounds generated. Examples of the current tone include phrases such as “Viral for a reason,” “Now we’re just getting started,” “A bold wing concept built for the way people order today,” and “Enough scrolling.”
5. Section numbering like “01 — The Lineup” and “02 — Culture” makes the site feel like a design case study, not a restaurant.
6. The same basket-of-wings cutout is reused as a major hero object and again near the final CTA, reducing impact.
7. The site leans too hard on near-black. ATL cyan and orange feel like accents instead of the brand owning the page.
8. The experience does not vary alignment enough. There should be centered moments, right-anchored moments, split-screen moments, full-bleed moments, horizontal moments, and asymmetrical moments.
9. The current design could plausibly belong to many trendy chicken concepts if the ATL logo were removed.
10. Static “reel-style” frames are not a substitute for real social content. Do not fake TikTok/Instagram UI.
11. The franchise story is important, but a large franchise section on the consumer homepage interrupts the food/order journey.
12. The customer path should be simpler: crave food -> choose category/flavor -> choose location -> order.
13. DoorDash attribution is repeated too visibly. Keep it honest, but let ATL remain the dominant brand.
14. Menu pricing should be treated as location-dependent and should not be presented as universal if the data is not being actively maintained.
15. The site needs more human/location context: storefronts, staff, customers, physical spaces, actual social media, packaging, sauce tosses, and restaurant energy.
16. The brand story is good but the wording needs to sound like a human brand voice, not agency copy.
17. The location experience should feel like a real chain locator, not just a grid of addresses.
18. The design needs fewer generic cards and more art-directed compositions.
19. Animations should create 3-4 memorable moments, not make every section animate similarly.
20. Mobile ordering should be the fastest path on the site.

## Desired direction
- More brand-led, less agency-led.
- More food appetite, less design-demo flexing.
- More color blocking and photography.
- More central/full-width compositions.
- Real ATL personality.
- Stronger order conversion.
- Stronger location discovery.
- Better visual variety.
- Shorter, sharper copy.
